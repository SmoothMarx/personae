## 🧩 Three lines, three slots

The lines are not rivals — each answers a different question. Shipping all three is correct; promoting two of them to the same slot is not.

| Line | Slot | Answers | Lives on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Sorted by who, not when.** | Tagline | How is it filed? | Under the name, everywhere |
| **Not a chat window. A contacts list.** | Sub-head | What does it replace? | Landing page, store listing |
| **One messenger. All your personae.** | Kicker | How many? | Body close, README opener |

The tagline carries the memory hook. The other two do the explaining, in either order.

The mobile README already carries a compatible fourth form — *"Your profiles as contacts. Their sessions as threads."* — and it may stay: it says the same thing in the same register, from the phone's point of view.

---

## 🎯 The claim

Every AI chat product files history **chronologically**: one chat box, one long list, `/new` when it gets muddy. Personae files **by correspondent**: a roster of profiles, each holding its own threads.

That is the whole differentiator, and it is structural — a competitor cannot copy the sentence without rebuilding their sidebar. Anything that does not express *partition* (many conversations that stay separate) or *ordering* (who, not when) is off-message, however nice it sounds.

---

## ✍️ Register rules

1. **Plain, feature-led, verb-first.** No metaphor, no theatre. The name is already the clever bit; the copy's job is to be understood.
2. **Say "who", not "multiple".** Quantity is a number; partition is a product. A pile of conversations is a mess — the promise is that many conversations stay *distinct and filed*.
3. **Name the pain it prevents.** "Nothing bleeds", "never have to reset", "not tangled" — all truer than "powerful" or "seamless".
4. **Agents are correspondents, not servants.** They get threads, unread badges, a place in the list. Not a prompt box, not a console, not a dashboard.
5. **Concrete nouns from the product itself** — contacts, threads, unread badge, pin, mute, archive, filed, sorted. Every one of them is a real thing in the pane.
6. **No hype adjectives.** No seamless, powerful, effortless, revolutionary, unlock, ecosystem. The README's own rule applies: wit yes, hype no.
7. **British spelling, consistently** — *sorted*, *organised*, *colour*, *behaviour*. Never mix in an American variant.

## 🚫 Settled against — do not relitigate

Wording tried and rejected, with the reason, so the argument does not restart:

| Rejected | Why |
| --- | --- |
| *…between you and your assistants* | Files the product in the "chat with your AI assistant" bucket; "assistants" softens the plurality and implies one-on-one service |
| *Maintain multiple conversations…* | "Maintain" is a chore verb; nobody wants maintenance |
| *…organized between profiles* | Wrong preposition — it is organised **by** profile |
| *A cast, not a console* | Theatre-pretentious if the surrounding page is serious; "console" only bites CLI users |
| *Every face gets its own thread* | Same sentence as "every agent gets a face" — shipping both cancels both |
| *As many personae as you need* | Pricing-tier cadence ("as many devices as you need"), and it implies personae are created in the app |
| *Organised by who, not when* | **Not rejected — shortened.** "Sorted" beats "organised": organised means tidied, sorted means ordered, and ordering is what the sidebar does |
